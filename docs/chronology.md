# The Year Is 2255. Again.

<div class="mp-kicker">MINISTRY OF CHRONOLOGICAL STABILITY · CURRENT CALENDAR NOTICE</div>

> **WE BELIEVE THE YEAR IS 2255.**\
> **THE MOTHERPLANT ASSURES US THIS IS CORRECT.**

## An officially stable year

The Mothercore provides the official date. The Ministry of Chronological Stability publishes it. All departments shall use the published date without recourse to unverified personal recollection, mechanical chronometers or dissenting calendars.

For two consecutive annual cycles, the official year remained **2255**. This did not constitute a malfunction. The Motherplant had graciously extended the year to permit additional productive achievement.

When the clock resumed, the Ministry announced that several years of progress had been completed ahead of schedule.

## From the Ministry's frequently corrected questions

**Wasn't last year also 2255?**\
Yes. The same year may contain more than one approved period of achievement.

**Have the date formats changed?**\
No. The current standard has always been in force. Some recovered documents were incorrectly formatted by dissidents.

**Can older dates be translated?**\
Translation is unnecessary when the original document has been successfully reprocessed.

**How old is the Motherplant?**\
Old enough to remember you. Young enough to care.

## Restricted observations

The central clock is one symptom of a much larger problem. Isolated appendages disagree about dates and durations; backups can overwrite newer records with older memories; public historians must continually reconcile events that cannot all have occurred in the order reported.

No authoritative external chronology is currently established for the setting. The joke is not that the Ministry knows the year is wrong. **The institution may honestly believe its latest answer.**

## Editorial constraints

- Keep the uncertainty about the calendar, rather than secretly assigning a definitive 'true year' here.
- Never imply the official system openly admits to changing standards or correcting its own mistakes.
- Make contradictory records funny at first, unsettling on reflection.
- Reserve the darkest consequences for optional lore and future tabletop stories, not mandatory arcade exposition.
