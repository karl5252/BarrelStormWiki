> **THE PEOPLE LOVE MOTHER. THE PEOPLE ALSO LOVE ONE ANOTHER.**

The Motherplant is not populated only by ministers, soldiers and victims. Below are deliberately small, incomplete human stories. None requires a twist.

## Vera, communal kitchen worker

"Every winter people complain the soup is thin. Then somebody brings mushrooms from the old growing beds, and somebody else finds a jar of onions, and somebody remembers how his mother used to make dumplings. They eat and stop complaining for a while. Of course I say the ingredients were provided by Mother. Some were. Some came from my cousin's garden. Is that a problem?"

## Sergeant Lev, recovery tanker

"My daughter made me a paper medal. She painted it gold and put a tiny red star on it. It tore when I climbed into the tank. I keep it behind the instrument glass. The Ministry gave me a real medal later. It's still in its box."

## Ilya, pipe mechanic

"You can tell a pipe is ill by the way it knocks. A healthy one has a rhythm. This one sounded like somebody knocking on a door for three nights. The central diagnostics said the pressure was ideal. So I repaired it without asking. Don't put that in the report."

## Dasha, student, age nine

"We made a cake for Mother. We couldn't give it to Her because the hatch said FOOD NOT ACCEPTED. So we ate it for Her. Teacher said that was acceptable devotion. It had beet jam inside."

## Stationmaster of Last Platform

"My father said the train was late. I told him the new timetable says it hasn't been built yet. He said he'd waited for it before I was born. Both of us had a good point."

## The unavailable interview

**INTERVIEWER:** Are you happy here?

**RESPONDENT:** Usually. My wife is here. My children. The school is good. When the pipes work, it's warm. We have friends.

**INTERVIEWER:** And when the pipes don't work?

**RESPONDENT:** Then we go to the neighbors. That's what people do.

**INTERVIEWER:** Do you trust Mother?

**RESPONDENT:** I trust my neighbors.
