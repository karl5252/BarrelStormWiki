# The Calendar of Gratitude

<div class="mp-kicker">MINISTRY OF MATERNAL WELFARE · APPROVED OBSERVANCES</div>

> **EVERY DAY IS MOTHER'S DAY. SOME DAYS ARE MORE OFFICIAL.**

An ordinary citizen experiences the Motherplant through ceremonies as much as through orders. These festivals are often sincerely loved: people share food, wear their best coats, and visit relatives along heated railways. The Ministry merely insists that enjoyment be documented.

## First Warmth

The anniversary of the first furnace ignition, celebrated on a date that has always been correct. Every district lights a small red lamp. Parents tell children the story of the First Winter: the surface froze, the lamps went dark, and Mother opened her arms beneath the earth.

At midnight, the distribution network briefly raises radiator temperatures. The warmth is called *Mother's Embrace*. Whether every district receives it is a matter for local gratitude officers.

**Community notice:** This year's First Warmth will occur twice to accommodate the second year of 2255. The festival has never been repeated.

## Mother's Day

Children prepare drawings of smiling reactors, paper flowers with turbine blades, and plasticine cooling towers. Gifts are recorded and placed in municipal intake hatches. Local folklore claims Mother keeps all of them. Workers at the intake office are forbidden to discuss storage capacity.

The official school song ends with the promise: *Mother hears every child.* In districts with faulty microphones, teachers sing it louder.

## The Parade of Returning Steel

Recovered tank crews drive past banners and choir stands while their vehicles are washed, repainted, and assigned triumphal ribbons. Public announcements emphasize the number of CORES recovered. The list of crews who did not return is replaced with a list of positions available for renewed service.

A surviving family may receive the medal before learning whether it belongs to their relative.

## Day of Reconciliation

An annual celebration of one hundred years of friendship with the designated neighboring state. When diplomatic designations change, the historical exhibits are reprocessed. Citizens are encouraged to donate any privately held photographs that contradict the officially uninterrupted friendship.

In the Year 2255, two opposing versions of the same commemorative banner were printed by the same factory. Both passed quality control.

## The Quiet Hour

Once per annual cycle, citizens sit silently while the local heating network is briefly shut down to honor those who built it. The silence lasts sixty minutes according to the Ministry clock. In the oldest sectors, households prepare coats and blankets ahead of the ceremony.

!!! note "Festival design principle"
    These rituals should feel comforting before they feel sinister. Most participants genuinely enjoy them. The horror comes when a ceremony continues after its purpose, witnesses, or beneficiaries have vanished.
