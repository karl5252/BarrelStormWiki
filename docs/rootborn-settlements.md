# Settlements Beneath the Roots

<div class="mp-kicker">DIRECTORATE OF HABITATION · SETTLEMENT REGISTER</div>

> **NO CITIZEN IS FAR FROM MOTHER. DISTANCE IS AN ADMINISTRATIVE CONDITION.**

## The inhabited network

Not every node beneath the crust is a military base. Some are crowded apartment galleries, school districts, rail junctions, communal kitchens, farms under electric light, repair yards, or hospitals built around a warm pipe. Citizens name the sound of their nearest root the way surface peoples name rivers.

The *Great Network* is a mechanical mycelium; it contains thousands of places that may never appear on a central map. Nobody in-world knows how large it is. All named settlements here are illustrative setting locations, not fixed arcade levels.

## Hearth Twelve

A residential node surrounding a thermal exchanger. Its people consider the distant pulse of a compressor to be a lucky sound. Couples marry beside the warmest vent. The night shift leaves bowls of soup for the workers who watch the pressure gauges.

The latest printed population register lists 4,208 residents. A handwritten correction in the margin reads: *Please stop sending school lunches for the east wing.*

## Junction of Three Mothers

Three huge pipes meet over a railway market. Each has a separate public address voice, accent, and maintenance schedule. Locals affectionately call them the Three Mothers and argue about which is the kindest. Central Administration insists there has only ever been one voice.

Once, the voices issued contradictory evacuation orders. Residents followed whichever one had kept their homes warm that winter.

## The Red Orchard

An underground agricultural district illuminated by aging grow lamps. Mushroom beds and root vegetables feed surrounding sectors. Its children's harvest festival includes wooden tanks painted red and gold. The Ministry lists its output as increasing annually; the gardeners quietly use last year's seed count because the official calendar has repeated itself.

## Last Platform

A small station on a rail line whose trains arrive less frequently with every generation. Families still wash the platform before a scheduled arrival. A grandmother keeps her late husband's timetable in a tin box; according to the official timetable, the service has never been interrupted.

## Bureaucratic geography

A place may disappear from the central register while its residents continue living there. Another may remain on official maps long after evacuation. Supply machines obey addresses, not testimony. The humane response is often neighbor-to-neighbor help, unofficial barter and local maintenance, all of which become technically unauthorized when recorded.
